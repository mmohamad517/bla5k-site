Phone calls are the last unconquered frontier of automation — until recently, no AI could reliably hold a conversation. **Vapi** changed that: it's a developer platform for building **voice AI agents** that answer and make calls, understand callers, and complete real tasks. From clinics booking appointments to support lines answering FAQs, Vapi powers the new generation of "AI receptionist" applications.

This guide covers what Vapi is, how it works, its pricing model, and who should use it.

## What is Vapi?

Vapi is a **platform for building voice AI agents**. Developers configure an agent — its system prompt, voice, and tools — attach a phone number, and Vapi handles the full audio pipeline in real time: converting speech to text, sending it to an AI model for reasoning, and speaking the response back with a natural voice.

It's the developer-facing layer beneath the "AI receptionist" apps you may have seen — the plumbing that makes voice automation possible.

## How Vapi works

1. **Create an agent** — define its instructions, personality, and knowledge.
2. **Connect tools** — calendar, CRM, booking APIs, or custom integrations.
3. **Attach a number** — or build the agent into your app with the SDK.
4. **Go live** — Vapi handles speech-to-text, AI reasoning, and text-to-speech during every call.

You're not training anything — you're configuring the AI's role and giving it the tools to act.

## Key features

### Real-time voice AI
Natural conversations with low latency, powered by leading LLMs.

### Phone and web calls
Agents handle incoming and outgoing calls; web voice widgets too.

### Tool integration
Connect calendars, CRMs, and APIs so agents can take real actions.

### Voice library
Pick from many natural voices or clone custom voices.

### Developer SDKs
Build voice agents into your own apps.

### No-code option
A builder and template library for simpler use cases.

## Who is Vapi for?

- **Developers** building voice automation products.
- **Businesses** automating phone support, bookings, and intake.
- **Startups** shipping AI receptionists and voice apps.
- **Agencies** building voice solutions for clients.

## Pricing

Vapi is **usage-based (paid)**: you pay per minute of voice AI conversation, with pricing varying by the AI models and voices you choose. A small free credit lets you test. Because it's metered, cost scales with call volume — cheap for light use, serious for call centers. Check vapi.ai for the current rate card.

## Advantages

- **Real conversations** — voice AI quality has crossed the usable line.
- **Tool-calling** — agents don't just chat; they book, check, and update.
- **Pay per use** — no heavy upfront subscription.
- **Developer-friendly** — SDKs, docs, and a config-first model.
- **Fast to launch** — a working agent can be live in an afternoon.

## Limitations and honest considerations

- **Per-minute costs add up** — high call volume is real money.
- **Still not human-perfect** — edge cases and accents can trip agents.
- **Setup quality matters** — good agents need careful prompts and tool config.
- **Regulation** — voice calls and recordings have legal rules; deploy responsibly.

## Alternatives and comparisons

Competitors include **Bland AI**, **Retell AI**, and **LiveKit Agents**. Vapi's edge: developer ergonomics and a strong voice model lineup. For text-based support chatbots, see [Chatbase](/tools/chatbase/). For voice recordings and noise removal, [Krisp](/tools/krisp/).

## The bottom line

Vapi is the platform making AI receptionists and voice automation real. If you build software — or run a business that answers phones — it's the fastest route from idea to a voice agent that books, answers, and routes calls. Start with the free test credit, build one agent around a single real task, and measure the calls it handles.

> **Tip:** Give your agent one clear job and a tight system prompt first — focused agents outperform generalists dramatically on real calls.
