The awkward part of using modern AI is the pile of apps: a ChatGPT subscription here, Claude there, Gemini somewhere else, each with its own window and its own limits. IntelliBar is a native macOS answer to that mess — one menu-bar app, one keyboard shortcut, and a single window that talks to whichever model you want, including local ones. No subscription, no usage caps, and your chat history stays on your machine.

## What is IntelliBar?

IntelliBar is a macOS AI assistant designed like Spotlight: press a shortcut and a command bar drops down anywhere in the system. Behind that bar sits a long list of models — GPT-4o, o1, and o3 mini, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek, Perplexity's models — plus local models running through Ollama. You connect your own API keys, and the app routes your prompt straight to the provider you pick.

Its positioning is deliberately different from consumer chat apps: instead of paying a flat subscription to one vendor, you pay the providers for what you actually use, or use local models at no cost.

## How IntelliBar works

1. **Install and open** — the app sits in the macOS menu bar.
2. **Press the shortcut** — a Spotlight-style bar appears from anywhere.
3. **Pick a model** — choose from the connected providers or a local model.
4. **Chat** — get an answer, compare responses across models, or use slash commands and custom instructions.

Productivity touches include comparing the same prompt across several models side by side, smart prompt completion, a command menu for quick actions, instant search over past chats, read-aloud, and voice input.

## Who is it for?

IntelliBar suits Mac users who live in the keyboard — developers, writers, researchers, and power users who want AI without switching windows. It is especially appealing to people who already hold API keys and resent paying for subscriptions on top, and to privacy-conscious users who want a local-model path.

It is less suited to beginners who want a zero-config account with included credits, or to teams that need shared workspaces and collaboration.

## Pricing

IntelliBar is free to evaluate; continued use requires a one-time license purchase, with no subscription. You bring your own API keys, so your recurring cost is whatever the model providers charge per token — often a few dollars a month for light use, versus $20 per subscription per vendor. Connect local models through Ollama and the runtime cost drops to zero.

## Alternatives and comparisons

IntelliBar competes with other macOS AI menu-bar clients (like RewriteBar and Kerlig) and with the official apps for each model. Against single-vendor apps, its advantage is breadth — one window for every major model and local ones — and its pay-per-use model avoids stacking subscriptions. Against other menu-bar clients, the differentiators are the model list, the open-source project, and features like side-by-side comparison. The trade-off is that it is Mac-only and expects you to manage your own API keys.

## Conclusion

If you already use several AI providers and want one fast, private entry point on your Mac, IntelliBar is a strong candidate — evaluate it for free, connect your keys, and run one real task through it. If you prefer a zero-setup web chat with built-in credits, a subscription app remains the simpler choice.
