Search for this tool and you will find pages — including older ones on this site — describing something called **"The AI Exchange."** That name is on its way out. The service still exists; it now operates as **AMP**. Understanding that rename is most of what you need before deciding whether to sign up.

## What it actually is

Not a marketplace, despite the name. It is a **membership community** built for business and product leaders trying to get AI adoption past the talking stage: a private Slack, weekly member-led events, curated listings of AI tools and use cases, and a newsletter.

The value on offer is **peer knowledge**, not software. You are not buying a tool — you are buying access to people already doing the work, and a curated feed so you spend less time hunting for what's worth trying.

## The rebrand, and why it matters to you

If you find "The AI Exchange" through a search, an old bookmark, or a link on a directory like this one, you are looking at the previous name. The organisation now runs as **AMP**, and current signup and membership pages live under that name.

This matters practically: if pricing, membership details, or the newsletter signup on an old-branded page look stale or broken, that is the rename, not the service shutting down. Look for AMP rather than assuming the old name has died along with the link.

## What's free vs. what's paid

**Free:** the newsletter. It covers curated AI tools and use cases without requiring membership.

**Paid, with a 7-day free trial:** the community itself — private Slack access, weekly member events, member-only content. Exact current pricing is not published in any source we could verify; check the live signup page during your trial, before it converts to a paid charge.

## Who actually gets value here

**Business and product leads**, not developers. The community is oriented around operations and adoption — how to actually roll AI into a team's workflow — not around writing code or evaluating model architecture. If you want technical depth, this is the wrong door.

**People who learn from peers rather than courses.** The pitch is real-time answers from people building right now, in a private Slack, over a static curriculum. If that format doesn't suit how you learn, the paid tier won't feel worth it regardless of content quality.

## The honest trade-off

A paid Slack community lives or dies on **who else is in it and how active they are** — something no outside review, including this one, can verify for you from the outside. The free trial exists specifically to let you check that before paying, and it is worth actually using for that purpose rather than skipping to signup.

If the free newsletter alone gives you enough tool discovery, there may be no reason to pay for the rest.

## Limitations and honest considerations

- **Name confusion is real** — expect outdated pages, old bookmarks, and directory listings (this one included, until now) referring to "The AI Exchange" when the live brand is AMP.
- **Value depends on community activity**, which you cannot assess from outside — use the trial.
- **Not technical** — a poor fit if you wanted engineering-level AI content.
- **Pricing is not public** — you will not know the real cost until you are inside the trial.

## The verdict

Worth the 7-day trial if you are a business or product lead trying to move AI adoption forward and you learn better from peers than from courses — but go in expecting AMP, not The AI Exchange, and confirm current pricing before the trial converts. If you only want a curated tool feed, the free newsletter may be all you actually need.

> **Before you sign up:** search "AMP community AI" rather than "The AI Exchange" — you'll land on the current version instead of a stale rebrand trail.
