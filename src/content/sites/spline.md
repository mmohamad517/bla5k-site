3D used to be the domain of heavyweight software and years of training. **Spline** changed that for the web: a 3D design tool that runs in your browser, where you build interactive 3D scenes, animate them, and publish them to any site with a link. This guide covers what Spline is, what you can build, and whether it's right for you.

## What is Spline?

Spline is a **browser-based 3D design tool** for creating interactive 3D experiences. It launched in 2020 with a clear goal: make 3D design as accessible as 2D design tools like Figma.

With Spline you can:

- **Model 3D scenes** — primitives, shapes, and objects
- **Apply materials** — colors, textures, lighting
- **Animate** — keyframe animations and auto-animations
- **Add interactions** — clicks, hovers, scroll triggers, physics
- **Collaborate** — real-time multi-user editing
- **Publish** — export to web, image, or video

It's become a favorite of web designers, product teams, and agencies looking to add that "wow" factor to landing pages and apps.

## How Spline works

Spline works like a design tool, not a 3D software:

1. **Open spline.design** in your browser — no install.
2. **Add objects** — drag in primitives (cubes, spheres) or import models.
3. **Style them** — materials, colors, and lighting.
4. **Animate** — set keyframes or use auto-animations.
5. **Add interactions** — wire clicks, hovers, and scrolls.
6. **Publish** — embed on your site or export.

The interface is visual and immediate — you see your scene render as you build it, with real-time collaboration if you invite teammates.

## Key features of Spline

### Browser-based and free to start

Runs entirely **in your browser** with a generous free plan — no powerful GPU required, no downloads.

### Interactive 3D for the web

Create scenes that **respond to clicks, hovers, scroll, and mouse movement** — genuinely interactive 3D, not static renders.

### Real-time collaboration

Work on scenes **together with your team** in real time, like a 3D version of Google Docs.

### Easy animation

Keyframe animation, **auto-animations, and physics** let you add motion without a timeline headache.

### One-click web publishing

Export a **shareable link or embed code** — your scene runs on any site, no plugins needed.

**Spline owns the web-3D niche** — interactive, browser-based, designer-friendly. **Blender** is for serious production 3D. **Figma** is for 2D UI. Each has its place; Spline is the one that makes 3D approachable for web designers.

## Who is Spline for?

- **Web designers** adding 3D flair to sites.
- **Product teams** creating interactive mockups.
- **Agencies** building memorable landing pages.
- **Creators** experimenting with 3D without heavy tools.

It's **not** for professional game development (Unity/Unreal/Blender are the tools), high-poly production work, or offline desktop modeling. For interactive web 3D, though, nothing is friendlier.

## How to get started with Spline

1. **Create a free account** at spline.design.
2. **Start from a template** or a blank scene.
3. **Add primitives** and play with materials and lighting.
4. **Try an auto-animation** — orbit, pulse, or float.
5. **Add an interaction** — make something clickable.
6. **Publish** — embed your first scene on a test page.

Start with a simple object and one animation — you'll have a shareable 3D scene in your first session.

Spline makes **interactive 3D design as easy as 2D design** — browser-based, collaborative, and built for the web. Whether it's a product mockup, an animated logo, or a fully interactive landing page, Spline lets designers create experiences that used to require specialized 3D teams.
