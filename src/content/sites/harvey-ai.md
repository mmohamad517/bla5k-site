## What is Harvey AI?

Harvey AI is a **generative AI platform designed specifically for the legal industry**. It helps lawyers handle the document-heavy work that dominates legal practice — contracts, briefs, memos, and research — with models trained and configured for legal accuracy.

Its core capabilities:

- **Contract drafting and review** — clauses, redlines, risk flags
- **Document analysis** — extract and summarize key terms at scale
- **Legal research** — find and reason over case law and authorities
- **Workflow integration** — fits into how firms actually work

Backed by major investors and used by top-tier law firms, Harvey has become the reference implementation of vertical AI for law.

## How Harvey works

The workflow is built for legal practice:

1. **Upload or connect documents** — contracts, briefs, discovery materials.
2. **Ask legal questions** — "flag all indemnification clauses," "summarize the termination terms."
3. **Harvey analyzes** — reads the documents with legal context.
4. **Get drafted or reviewed work** — clauses, summaries, and risk assessments.
5. **Lawyer reviews** — professional judgment stays with the attorney.

The key design choice is legal specificity: Harvey is tuned for legal language and tasks rather than being a general chatbot wearing a law degree.

## Key features of Harvey

### Contract analysis at scale

Draft, review, and extract from **contracts and agreements** — including identifying unusual or risky clauses across hundreds of pages.

### Legal research

Search and reason over **case law and legal authorities**, with citation-aware answers for legal arguments.

### Document-heavy workflows

Handle **large document sets** — discovery, due diligence, M&A — with extraction and summarization that saves weeks of manual review.

### Enterprise-grade security

Built for **law firm requirements**: data isolation, access controls, and compliance — essential for confidential client work.

### Integration with practice

Designed to **fit existing legal workflows** rather than forcing a new process on attorneys.
