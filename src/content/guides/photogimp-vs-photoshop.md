# PhotoGIMP vs Photoshop (2026): Complete Comparison & Honest Verdict

If you know Photoshop but hate the subscription, **PhotoGIMP** is the most popular escape hatch in the free-photo-editing world — a free patch that makes GIMP look and feel exactly like Adobe Photoshop. But is it actually a Photoshop replacement, or just a familiar skin on a different engine? This comparison settles it with real numbers: price, features, AI tools, and the honest cases where each one wins. It doubles as the **GIMP vs Photoshop** answer, since PhotoGIMP sits on top of GIMP and shares its engine. Short answer: **PhotoGIMP for $0 with muscle-memory ease, Photoshop for professional power and AI.**

Both are real, full-featured editors — the gap is **power and price**, not quality of the basics. The sections below tell you exactly when free is enough and when Adobe earns its bill.

## What PhotoGIMP Actually Is

**PhotoGIMP** ([full review](/tools/photogimp/)) is not an app — it's a free, open-source **patch** from the Brazilian team Diolinux that reshapes GIMP's interface to mirror Photoshop:

- **Tools in Photoshop's positions** — no more hunting for where everything moved
- **Adobe-style keyboard shortcuts** — your muscle memory carries over
- **Optimized workspace** — a cleaner, larger working area by default
- **Familiar icons and branding**

You install GIMP first (also free, from [gimp.org](https://www.gimp.org)), then drop the PhotoGIMP files into GIMP's config folder. That's it. It changes configuration, not the engine — which is both its genius and its honest limitation.

## Where Photoshop Is Genuinely Ahead

Being honest: if the only difference were price, every professional would switch. These are the real reasons many keep paying:

### Adobe Firefly AI (Generative Fill & Remove)
Photoshop has **native generative AI** — select an area, type a prompt, and Firefly generates content directly on the canvas, including object removal and image expansion. GIMP has no native AI of this caliber. Workaround: pair free GIMP/PhotoGIMP with free AI tools like [EzRemove](/tools/ezremove/) for background removal and [Remini](/tools/remini/) for enhancement — good results, just not integrated.

### Camera Raw
Photoshop's built-in **Camera Raw** gives photographers a seamless, non-destructive raw workflow. GIMP relies on external raw processors (Darktable, RawTherapee) — workable, but a separate step with its own interface.

### Native CMYK & Print Workflows
For **professional print** — brochures, packaging, anything going to a commercial press — Photoshop handles CMYK and spot colors natively. GIMP has limited native CMYK support; print professionals will hit the wall.

### Smart Objects & Advanced Compositing
Photoshop's **Smart Objects** keep transformations and effects non-destructive at scale. GIMP's non-destructive editing is more limited, and heavy professional compositing workflows are smoother in Adobe.

### The Ecosystem
Lightroom, Illustrator, Premiere Pro, font licensing, cloud collaboration — Adobe's ecosystem is a real workflow for many professionals. That's not a feature of one app; it's a system.

## Where PhotoGIMP Wins

- **Price**: $0, forever, no trials or watermarks.
- **Linux**: Photoshop doesn't run on Linux at all; GIMP + PhotoGIMP is the obvious choice.
- **Familiarity for free**: if you know Photoshop, you keep your shortcuts and layout at zero cost.
- **Open source**: GPL-3.0 — inspect, modify, and trust it. Adobe is a closed black box.
- **Lightweight**: GIMP is lighter than Photoshop on modest hardware.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is PhotoGIMP as good as Photoshop?

No — and that's the honest answer. PhotoGIMP makes GIMP *feel* like Photoshop (same layout, shortcuts, and workspace), but the engine underneath is still GIMP's. Photoshop has features GIMP simply lacks: Adobe Firefly generative AI, Camera Raw, native CMYK for print, and deep Smart Object workflows. For everyday layer-based editing, the free combo covers most needs; for professional print work and AI-assisted editing, Photoshop remains ahead.

### Is PhotoGIMP completely free?

Yes, completely. PhotoGIMP is free and open source under the GPL-3.0 license, and GIMP is also free and open source. The whole stack costs $0 forever — no trials, no subscriptions, no watermarks. Compare that to Photoshop's $22.99/month (single app) or $19.99/month (Photography plan) with only a 7-day trial.

### Can PhotoGIMP open Photoshop files (PSD)?

PhotoGIMP itself doesn't change file handling, but GIMP — which it patches — opens .PSD files including layers, masks, and blend modes. Compatibility is good for everyday use, but some advanced Photoshop-specific features (smart objects, certain effects, adjustment layers edge cases) don't carry over perfectly. If PSD fidelity is critical, PhotoPea or Photoshop itself is safer.

### Is GIMP vs Photoshop the same question as PhotoGIMP vs Photoshop?

Essentially yes. PhotoGIMP is a layer on top of GIMP — the engine, tools, and file support are all GIMP's, so anything true of "GIMP vs Photoshop" is true here with the added benefit that PhotoGIMP makes the interface familiar. If you're comparing pure editors: GIMP + PhotoGIMP is the free Photoshop-alike; plain GIMP is the same engine with a different layout.

### Does PhotoGIMP have Photoshop's AI tools?

No. Photoshop has Adobe Firefly built in: generative fill, generative expand, and AI object removal directly on the canvas, plus Content-Aware fill. GIMP has no native AI of that caliber. You can approximate some effects with plugins like Resynthesizer, or use free AI tools like EzRemove for background removal — but it's not the same integrated experience.

### What is the biggest difference between PhotoGIMP and Photoshop?

Price versus power. PhotoGIMP costs $0 and gives you a Photoshop-like interface on top of GIMP's capable open-source engine. Photoshop costs $19.99-$69.99/month and gives you the industry-standard tool with AI features, Camera Raw, native CMYK, and a vast plugin ecosystem. If you're a student, hobbyist, or freelancer watching budget, PhotoGIMP wins. If you work in professional print, photography, or need Adobe's AI — Photoshop justifies its cost.

### Should I switch from Photoshop to PhotoGIMP?

If you mainly retouch photos, edit layers, and make web/social graphics, try PhotoGIMP first — you'll keep your keyboard shortcuts and layout for free. Keep Photoshop if you depend on generative AI, raw photography workflows (Camera Raw/Lightroom), native CMYK for print, or collaborate inside the Adobe ecosystem. Many people run both: PhotoGIMP for daily free work, Photoshop for professional deliverables.

## The Bottom Line

There is no single winner — there's the right tool for your wallet and your workflow. **PhotoGIMP + GIMP** is the smartest $0 move in photo editing: a familiar Photoshop-like interface over a capable open-source engine, especially unbeatable on Linux. **Photoshop** keeps the crown for professionals who need Adobe's AI (Firefly), Camera Raw, native CMYK print workflows, and the ecosystem — and it's the only place those live.
