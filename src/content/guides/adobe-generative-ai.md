# Adobe's Generative AI in 2026: What Firefly Actually Costs and Does

Search "Adobe generative AI" and you'll land on a genuinely confusing picture: Photoshop has a "Generative Fill" button, Illustrator has "Generative Recolor," Adobe Express has AI templates, and somewhere in the marketing is a name — Firefly — that ties them all together. This guide untangles it: what Firefly actually is, where you'll run into it, what's free, and what the credit system really costs you.

## Firefly is the engine, not a single app

The short version: **Firefly is Adobe's family of generative AI models**, and it powers a specific feature inside almost every major Adobe app rather than existing as one standalone product you buy once.

- **Photoshop** — Generative Fill (add or replace content in a selection) and Generative Expand (extend a canvas beyond its original edges)
- **Illustrator** — Generative Recolor (recolor vector artwork by describing a palette or mood) and text-to-vector generation
- **Adobe Express** — AI-generated templates, backgrounds, and one-click image edits, aimed at non-designers
- **Premiere Pro** — Generative Extend, which lengthens a video or audio clip by generating a few extra seconds that match the original
- **Firefly (the standalone app)** — a browser-based tool for text-to-image, text effects, and generative fill without opening any other Adobe product

If you only ever open the Firefly web app directly, you're using one small slice of what "Adobe generative AI" actually covers. Most people encounter it accidentally, mid-edit, inside a tool they already had open.

## What's new in 2026: you can pick the model

The biggest change this year is that Photoshop's Generative Fill and Generative Expand are no longer locked to Adobe's own Firefly model. You now choose per task:

| Model | Best for | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Firefly** (Adobe) | Client and commercial work | Adobe's own IP-indemnification terms apply |
| **Google Gemini 3** | Keeping a character or subject consistent across multiple edits | Less predictable licensing terms than Firefly |
| **Black Forest Labs FLUX.2 Pro** | Photorealistic results and rendering legible text in-image | Not Adobe's own model — same indemnification caveat as Gemini |

Firefly Image 5, the latest version of Adobe's own model, also added a **Reference Images** mode (match a specific visual source instead of describing it in words) and a **Harmonize** tool that blends a pasted-in element by matching the surrounding lighting and shadows automatically — genuinely useful for compositing work that used to take manual dodge-and-burn passes.

Whichever model you pick, generations draw from the same Firefly credit balance tied to your account — the model choice changes the output, not the billing.

## What's actually free

This is the part most people searching "Adobe generative AI" want to know, and the honest answer is: **partially, and it depends which door you walk through.**

- **Adobe Express (free tier)** — includes a limited allowance of Firefly-powered templates and one-click AI edits, no credit card required. This is the most generous genuinely-free entry point.
- **Firefly web app** — new accounts get a monthly allotment of free generative credits before you hit a paywall. Fine for testing the tool casually; not enough for regular production work.
- **Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere Pro** — Generative Fill, Recolor, and Extend all require an active subscription to that app (or Creative Cloud), which bundles in a monthly Firefly credit allowance. There is no free way to use these features *inside* the desktop apps — the free tier only exists on the Express and standalone-Firefly side.

If your goal is "generate a few AI images for a social post," Express's free tier likely covers you. If your goal is "use Generative Fill to retouch photos in Photoshop," budget for a paid plan — there's no workaround.

## Adobe Firefly pricing (2026)

| Plan | Price | Credits/month | Fits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Firefly Standard | $9.99/mo | 2,000 | Casual, personal use |
| Firefly Pro | $29.99/mo | 7,000 | Freelancers and regular content creation |
| Firefly Premium | $199.99/mo | 50,000 | Agencies and high-volume production |

Credits are also available bundled into Creative Cloud Photography ($9.99/mo) and single-app Photoshop subscriptions — if you already pay for Photoshop, check your plan's included allowance before buying a separate Firefly plan, since you may already have credits going unused.

## Is it actually "commercially safe"?

Adobe's whole pitch for Firefly, versus a tool that scraped the open web, is that it's trained on **licensed Adobe Stock content, public domain material, and other content Adobe holds rights to** — not on images pulled from the internet without permission. That's a real and meaningful distinction if you're a business worried about copyright exposure in client-facing work, and some Firefly plans come with IP indemnification from Adobe as a result.

The honest caveat: that indemnification is specifically for Firefly-generated output. If you switch Photoshop's Generative Fill to the Gemini 3 or FLUX.2 Pro model instead (see above), you're using someone else's model with its own, separately-defined licensing terms — read the fine print for whichever model you actually generate with before you use the result commercially.

## Who should actually use this, and who shouldn't

**Good fit:**
- Photoshop or Illustrator users who want to extend, fill, or recolor existing work without leaving their usual editor
- Marketing and social teams using Express who need fast, on-brand visuals without a design background
- Agencies who specifically need Adobe's commercial-safety and indemnification terms for client deliverables

**Not the right tool if:**
- You want the single best raw image-generation quality regardless of ecosystem — dedicated generators built purely around a state-of-the-art model can outperform Firefly on pure prompt-to-image results
- You need genuinely free, unlimited generation — Adobe's free tiers are real but capped, by design
- You're not already inside the Adobe ecosystem — paying for Creative Cloud just to access Firefly is a poor trade if you don't otherwise use Photoshop or Illustrator

## The bottom line

"Adobe generative AI" isn't a single product to sign up for — it's Firefly, threaded through Photoshop, Illustrator, Express, and Premiere Pro as a feature inside tools you may already own. Adobe Express's free tier is the real no-cost entry point; Photoshop's Generative Fill is genuinely useful but requires a paid plan either way. The 2026 addition of choosing between Firefly, Gemini 3, and FLUX.2 Pro inside Photoshop means you're no longer locked to one model's strengths — worth knowing before you assume "Adobe's AI" means just one thing.
